* Evidence rules
- DOCUMENTED: architecture/claim is stated by an inspected primary paper,
  proceedings page, official documentation, or exact repository source.
- REPOSITORY DESIGN: visible in inspected source/docs but not independently
  executed by this book.
- UPSTREAM-REPORTED: benchmark/result reported by authors; not reproduced here.
- TESTED: reserved for book-owned code actually executed under a recorded gate.
- UNTESTED: proposed experiment, integration, or behavior not executed here.

No paper benchmark was run during this pass.

* Primary architecture sources
** PAL -- imperative program handoff
Source: [cite:@gao-pal-2023].

DOCUMENTED:
- the LLM generates programs as intermediate reasoning steps;
- solution execution is delegated to a runtime such as a Python interpreter;
- the paper evaluates program-aided reasoning across multiple reasoning tasks.

Interpretation used by book:
- runtime execution can remove arithmetic/operational work from token generation;
- runtime correctness does not establish semantic correctness of the generated
  program relative to the source problem;
- arbitrary generated program execution is a wider authority surface than this
  book's closed capability/data interfaces.

UPSTREAM-REPORTED:
- all accuracy and state-of-the-art comparisons in the paper.

UNTESTED:
- PAL code, prompts, providers, datasets, and benchmark reproduction in this
  repository.

** Faithful Chain-of-Thought -- symbolic chain then deterministic solver
Source: [cite:@lyu-faithful-cot-2023].

DOCUMENTED:
- two stages: natural-language query -> symbolic reasoning chain, then symbolic
  chain -> answer with a deterministic solver;
- the paper frames the resulting chain as faithful to the computation producing
  the final answer.

Book boundary:
- execution/explanation faithfulness is not automatically semantic faithfulness
  of the translation from source natural language.

UPSTREAM-REPORTED:
- benchmark improvements and accuracy figures.

** SatLM -- declarative specification then automated solver
Source: [cite:@ye-satlm-2023].

DOCUMENTED:
- the model generates a declarative task specification instead of an imperative
  program;
- an off-the-shelf automated theorem prover/solver derives the answer;
- the paper explicitly scopes solver correctness to the parsed specification.

Book boundary:
- this source directly supports the formalization-bottleneck distinction: a
  solver can be correct relative to an incorrect or incomplete translation.

UPSTREAM-REPORTED:
- comparative benchmark gains.

** LINC -- semantic parser to first-order logic
Source: [cite:@olausson-linc-2023].

DOCUMENTED:
- the LLM is used as a semantic parser for premises and conclusions;
- translated expressions are first-order logic;
- an external theorem prover performs the deductive inference.

Book boundary:
- semantic parsing and theorem proving are separate failure stages;
- final correctness can fail because of translation even if the prover behaves
  correctly on its actual input.

UPSTREAM-REPORTED:
- ProofWriter/FOLIO performance and model comparisons.

** Logic-LM -- solver feedback into formalization repair
Source: [cite:@pan-logic-lm-2023].

DOCUMENTED:
- the model translates natural-language problems into symbolic formulations;
- deterministic symbolic solvers perform inference;
- a self-refinement module uses solver error messages to revise symbolic
  formalizations.

Book boundary:
- error-driven repair turns a pipeline into a stateful loop;
- the loop needs frozen request/source identity, attempt IDs, bounded retries,
  and tests against weakening/removing difficult requirements.

UPSTREAM-REPORTED:
- all benchmark deltas and effectiveness claims.

** LLM+P -- natural language to PDDL to classical planner
Source: [cite:@liu-llmp-2023].

DOCUMENTED:
- natural-language planning problem is translated to PDDL;
- a classical planner solves the formal planning problem;
- the resulting plan is translated back to natural language.

Book boundary:
- planner quality does not repair a wrong initial state, goal, precondition,
  effect, type, or cost in generated PDDL;
- the LLM-to-planner boundary belongs in this book, while classical planning
  algorithm depth remains in =symbolic-ai-prolog=.

UPSTREAM-REPORTED:
- feasibility/optimality benchmark results.

** Explanation-Refiner -- theorem-prover validation and feedback
Source: [cite:@quan-explanation-refiner-2024].

DOCUMENTED:
- LLMs generate/formalize explanatory sentences and candidate inference
  strategies;
- theorem provers are used to validate logical explanation properties;
- formal feedback is used for refinement.

Book boundary:
- answer correctness, formal explanation validity, proof/source provenance, and
  natural-language readability require separate evidence.

UPSTREAM-REPORTED:
- evaluation results and paper-level quality claims.

** LLM-Modulo -- generate/test/critique with external verifiers
Source: [cite:@kambhampati-llm-modulo-2024].

DOCUMENTED:
- position paper argues against treating LLMs either as standalone reliable
  planners/reasoners or merely one-way format translators;
- proposes tighter bidirectional integration with external model-based
  verifiers/critics in an LLM-Modulo framework.

Book boundary:
- critic identity and hardness must be explicit;
- a critic bank needs a host-owned acceptance predicate;
- repair feedback does not grant new authority;
- a passing incomplete verifier can still be gamed by a degenerate candidate.

UPSTREAM-REPORTED / not adopted as local fact:
- empirical claims from cited/related planning work.

** Reliable Reasoning Beyond Natural Language -- Prolog deductive runtime
Source: [cite:@borazjanizadeh-prolog-2024].

DOCUMENTED:
- the LLM is prompted to encode relevant information as logical code statements;
- Prolog performs iterative explicit deductive reasoning.

Book boundary:
- this is strong evidence that Prolog has been used as the actual symbolic
  computation layer in an LLM integration;
- it does not justify executing arbitrary model-generated source in this book's
  production-facing teaching architecture;
- benchmark results stay upstream-reported.

UPSTREAM-REPORTED:
- GSM8K, Navigate, and NLR results.

** CaRing -- Prolog search logs to human-readable reasoning proofs
Source: [cite:@yang-caring-2025].

DOCUMENTED:
- customized Prolog interpreters are used as the symbolic reasoning component;
- intermediate search logs are accessed/interpreted into human-readable
  reasoning proofs;
- the paper conditions the proof reliability claim on correct translation of
  problem descriptions into Prolog representations.

Book boundary:
- proof provenance must remain separate from source/formalization provenance;
- deterministic proof artifacts can explain symbolic execution without proving
  that the source translation was correct.

UPSTREAM-REPORTED:
- answer/proof accuracy improvements.

** Recursive Language Models
Source: [cite:@zhang-rlm].

DOCUMENTED in existing research baseline:
- treats long context as an external environment available to programmatic
  inspection;
- uses recursive language-model calls over selected subproblems.

Book boundary:
- RLM is a different family from translate-and-solve theorem-prover pipelines;
- recursion needs explicit depth/call/concurrency/budget and duplicate-work
  accounting before the book's capstone can make strong claims.

UPSTREAM-REPORTED:
- paper task/benchmark results.

* Cross-system synthesis used by Chapters 20--24
** Family 1 -- program-aided execution
PAL demonstrates model-produced imperative programs executed by a runtime.
Primary audit question: what authority does generated program text receive?

** Family 2 -- symbolic/declarative solver handoff
Faithful CoT and SatLM move final solving into deterministic/symbolic components.
Primary audit question: what exactly is guaranteed relative to the translated
artifact?

** Family 3 -- semantic parser to theorem prover
LINC makes translation to FOL explicit. Logic-LM adds feedback-driven repair.
Primary audit question: how are mistranslation, malformed formalization, solver
result, and repair separated in evidence?

** Family 4 -- formal planner handoff
LLM+P translates natural language to PDDL for a classical planner.
Primary audit question: did the formal planning model preserve source state,
actions, invariants, and goals?

** Family 5 -- verifier-guided iterative systems
Logic-LM, Explanation-Refiner, and LLM-Modulo motivate bounded feedback loops.
Primary audit question: is verifier feedback typed and is the acceptance policy
complete enough to resist trivial verifier gaming?

** Family 6 -- Prolog-backed reasoning/proofs
Reliable Reasoning and CaRing put Prolog in the concrete reasoning path.
Primary audit question: can proof provenance be tied to accepted source-backed
premises without allowing arbitrary generated Prolog execution?

** Family 7 -- recursive externalized inference
RLM plus the current =prolog-rlm= implementation evidence motivate the later
capstone.
Primary audit question: how are recursive authority, budgets, duplicate work,
context access, and acceptance bounded?

* Manuscript consequences
Chapters 20--24 should not become a chronological paper survey. They are drafted
around architectural mechanisms:
1. taxonomy by authority transition;
2. formalization/semantic-parsing bottleneck;
3. verifier-guided repair;
4. Prolog proof/provenance and bounded recursive control;
5. replication/evaluation discipline.

All paper performance remains upstream-reported until the evaluation project
actually runs a pinned reproduction.
