#+title: Offline enrolled-equipment beacon lab

* Scope and status
This is a standard-library Python teaching model with fictional equipment,
receivers, operator enrollment, signal strengths, clocks, coverage and positions.
It opens no hardware, sockets, packet capture, scanner, account or live service.
It installs nothing. It models no person. Do not point it at a private-person
dataset or treat it as a field-ready collector or positioning service.

The inference implementation is book-local course code, not a StarIntel service.
=fixture_data.py= contains course DTOs; only =fixtures/*.json= are canonical
StarIntel documents. The strict fixture test imports the real pinned SDK and
fails if the required SDK or format checker is unavailable.

Authoring status: UNTESTED until the commands below actually run in the
target environment. Assertions are authored expected behavior, not recorded
experimental results. =run_lab.py= prints the answer key produced by that
execution. Do not copy a numerical result into the manuscript or change a
claim to TESTED before checking the run log and exact commit.

* Run without installing anything
From this directory, with an existing Python 3 environment:

#+begin_src sh
python3 -m unittest discover -s . -p 'test_lab.py' -v
python3 run_lab.py > owned-beacon-report.json
#+end_src

The inference code and its 23 tests use only Python's standard library.
The additional three fixture-validation tests require the already provisioned
=starintel-doc= checkout at =6aa8051dc7ea560943a60a25e1d600467f2778bc= and
=jsonschema[format]==4.26.0=, as in the existing canonical-records practicum:

#+begin_src sh
bash run-tests /path/to/pinned/starintel-doc
#+end_src

The runner verifies the SDK commit, tracked-file cleanliness and vendored
schema closure offline before all tests. It does not clone, install, update,
regenerate schemas or write product repositories. =PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1=
also prevents imports from adding cache files to the SDK checkout.
The report is printed only after the tests succeed.

* What is independently known
- An operator supplies stable asset IDs and explicit time-bounded radio-alias
  bindings. These entries are fictional enrollment records, not inference.
- Radio aliases are not authentication. A copied known alias is outside this
  toy model's threat guarantees. Unknown aliases remain unlinked; similar RSSI,
  address format, timing or payload never merges identities.
- Receiver position is instrument context. Fixed receivers occupy =(0,0)= and
  =(20,0)= metres. The owned rover has a =rover-west= pose at =(2,0)= for
  =[0,150)= and a =rover-east= pose at =(18,0)= for =[150,500)=.
- Coordinates are in =fictional-workshop-grid-metres=. They are not geographic
  latitude/longitude. The separate canonical geo-point/location fixtures are
  schema-only invented examples and are never inputs to zone classification.
- All raw receiver clocks already have a supplied transform into one
  simulation timescale. They are not arbitrary platform timestamps. Applying
  this subtraction to an uncalibrated monotonic-since-boot device clock would
  be invalid.

* Pipeline and course decisions
The exercises preserve raw receiver time and receipt time separately.
Corrected time is =receiver_time_s - clock_offset_s=. A supplied clock-error
bound gives a closed possible-observation interval. That whole interval must
fit exactly one explicit identity binding and one dated receiver pose.
Future observations, excessive uncertainty and stale samples are rejected.
Conservative age uses the interval's earliest possible observation time.

Deduplication groups the complete supplied session by =(receiver_id,sample_id)=.
An identical payload with a later receipt time is a replay of that supplied
sample identity. Conflicting payloads under one identity quarantine the whole
key, regardless of input order. This says nothing about whether ordinary BLE
advertisements authenticate freshness. Identical payloads with different
sample IDs are not automatically declared the same transmitter event.

The baseline fits a median RSSI fingerprint for each zone/receiver/pose from
calibration rows only. A receiver at a new pose needs separate calibration.
The test cases are separately authored and cannot be passed to =fit=.
They are synthetic holdout fixtures, not a representative real-world sample.

The intentionally explicit course gates are:
- Maximum conservative sample age: 15 s
- Maximum clock uncertainty: 2 s
- Maximum outer uncertainty span for a receiver pair: 4 s
- At least two distinct receivers with separation at least 10 m
- Winning mean absolute fingerprint residual at most 8 dB
- Margin between the two best mean residuals at least 6 dB

These are policy knobs for the exercise, not established physical constants.
Separation does not by itself establish real RF coverage or multilateration.
Residual and margin are scores, not probabilities or calibrated confidence.
There is no RSSI-to-distance, coordinate solve, device GPS output or metre-error
benchmark in this lab.

A health entry asserts the receiver was scheduled and healthy throughout the
four-second reporting frame. Missing health means unknown coverage. Even
adequate synthetic receiver coverage plus nondetection does not establish that
equipment was absent, off or unused. One accepted receiver sample can contribute
to visibility while still being insufficient for a zone estimate.

* Work through the examples
1. Inspect the two stable asset IDs and the three explicit alias bindings.
   Remove the second cart binding: the rotated alias must stop resolving.
   There is no automatic rotation-linking routine.
2. Compare the raw west/east receiver clocks with corrected observation time
   and receipt time. Widen an uncertainty interval until it crosses the
   rover-pose or enrollment boundary; the sample must be withheld.
3. Run the held-out cases. Find successful static and moving-receiver examples,
   a low-margin case, an out-of-calibration case, stale evidence, poor receiver
   separation, unknown coverage and an unlisted alias.
4. Find the deliberately misleading accepted fingerprint. It exists so the
   learner must report errors among accepted estimates as well as abstentions.
   Do not tune the calibration or thresholds after inspecting this test label.
5. Inspect the cart timeline. Its authored scene is west at reports 100/110,
   east at 120/130, a coverage gap at 140, an unlisted alias at 150, west with
   an explicitly enrolled rotated alias at 160/170, covered nondetection at
   180, west at 190/200 and east at 210/220.
6. Trace the two-result confirmation rule. Every confirmation requires new
   receiver/sample support. A repeated evaluation of the same sighting must
   not count twice. Unknown results and gaps greater than 15 s reset
   continuity; events record endpoint reports only and never draw a path.
7. Compare the cart and work-light summaries. Window visibility and unique
   receiver-sample support are different denominators. Several receivers
   seeing one transmission are not several equipment-use events.

* Read the generated answer key honestly
=held_out_evaluation= reports all test cases, accepted estimates, abstentions,
wrong accepted estimates, availability, error conditional on acceptance,
correct results over all cases and synthetic adequate-coverage rate.
A zero denominator produces JSON =null=, not a reassuring zero error rate.

=equipment_summaries= separates covered windows, visible covered windows,
covered nondetection, unknown coverage, zone availability and unique accepted
receiver-sample counts. These describe instrumentation visibility and
classification opportunities. They do not establish equipment operation,
daily routines, occupants, social relationships or a person's habits.

The two-result transition output distinguishes =acquired= from =changed=.
Reacquisition after a gap is not a transition across that gap. Endpoints do
not supply a route, speed, dwell duration or continuous occupancy.

* Canonical fixture boundary
=fixtures/observation.json= preserves an original receiver sample, a corrected
=observedAt=, a receipt =collectedAt= and receiver-local grid context under
=extensions.course=. Its =observerId= is the receiver asset and =subjectId=
is the enrolled equipment. Receiver position never becomes a device position.

=fixtures/analysis.json= is an authored single-receiver abstention example,
with =inputIds=, =findings=, =conclusions=, =limitations=,
=uncertaintySources= and =unresolved=. The test checks it against the lab's
single-receiver case. It contains no invented top-level zone/RSSI fields.
The fixture's =UNTESTED= claim label describes its authoring status; the actual
test log supplies execution evidence.
