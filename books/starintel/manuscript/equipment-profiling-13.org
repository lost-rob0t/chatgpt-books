* Chapter -- Profiling Enrolled Equipment
:PROPERTIES:

:END:

An equipment profile summarizes what a defined collection process observed
about an enrolled asset. It can help answer practical questions: was the
laboratory beacon visible when its receivers were operating, did its observed
zone change, did ingestion become delayed, or did a new firmware configuration
change the collected data?

The profile must retain its measurement conditions. A table of signal strengths
without receiver, pose, coverage and time context can make a receiver outage
look like an equipment failure.

This chapter continues the fictional owned-beacon lab. The subjects are
enrolled laboratory equipment, not people. No profile in the exercise describes
a person's identity, home, workplace, habits or intentions.

** Define the profile's purpose and unit

Choose the unit before aggregating. Useful units include:

- one enrolled asset during one test session;
- one receiver and pose during a coverage interval;
- one hardware/configuration combination during calibration;
- one zone-estimation method on a held-out evaluation set.

A profile mixing these units is hard to interpret. An asset moving between two
receivers can show an RSSI change even when its transmitter behavior remains
constant. A receiver moving on the cart can show a similar change while the
asset remains stationary.

Write a profile contract:

#+begin_example
Subject: enrolled asset in the fictional laboratory registry
Window: a named experiment interval
Purpose: summarize visibility and supported zone changes
Inputs: accepted observations plus receiver coverage and pose history
Exclusions: unknown bindings, conflicting duplicates, unsupported clock/pose data
Output: counts, denominators, uncertainty and evidence links
#+end_example

The profile is a derived analysis. Give it its own identity, processing version
and input references so a later implementation can reproduce or replace it.

** Start with a data-quality profile

Before asking what the equipment did, ask what happened to the data.

Retain counts for:

1. Raw received records.
2. Exact duplicate records.
3. Conflicting observation identities.
4. Unknown or out-of-interval enrollment bindings.
5. Invalid clock, RSSI or receiver-pose records.
6. Accepted unique observations.
7. Windows with usable collection coverage.
8. Windows with a supported zone result.
9. Windows that remain stale, uncovered or otherwise unknown.

These categories need clear rules. If one row fails multiple checks, specify
whether a primary rejection reason is recorded or several reason flags are
counted. Do not present overlapping reason counts as though they add up to
the total.

A deduplicated observation can contribute to more than one analytical view,
but it does not become a new observation each time it is displayed. Keep
observation counts separate from window-result counts. Reused support in
adjacent windows is correlated evidence, even when each displayed label is
individually well formed.

The chapter before this one also required new evidence for repeated-state
confirmation. Recomputing a label twice from the same samples must not
manufacture a second independent confirmation.

** Use coverage-aware visibility

A raw count answers how many records arrived. It does not answer how reliably
the equipment was observed.

Compare two fictional sessions. The first receiver was operational throughout
the session. The second was offline for half of it. Ten sightings in each
session do not establish equal detection behavior.

Define the denominator alongside the numerator. For example:

#+begin_example
observation visibility fraction =
  covered windows with at least one accepted observation
  / windows meeting the declared coverage rule

zone-output availability =
  declared evaluation windows with an accepted zone
  / all declared evaluation windows, including coverage gaps
#+end_example

These are different quantities. A beacon may be observed but lack enough
receiver support for a zone estimate. A window may have coverage but contain
an unrecognized address that cannot be assigned to an enrolled asset.

Also report wall-clock coverage. Excluding uncovered windows from a conditional
fraction should not hide a collection system that was unavailable most of the
time.

For every percentage, preserve the integer counts. "Ten of twelve covered
windows" is inspectable. A percentage with no denominator conceals both scale
and selection.

Our course parameters are not a product guarantee. The tests define what
counts as coverage and eligibility for the fictional dataset. A real collector
needs operational evidence that its radio, scan configuration, clock and pose
were usable.

** Advertising cadence is an observation of a sampling system

A dedicated beacon may be configured to advertise on an interval, but a receiver
does not necessarily report every transmission. Transport loss, scan windows,
filtering, scheduling and platform behavior affect the observed sequence.

The Bluetooth primer describes advertising timing and repeated broadcast
behavior. Android's permission guidance also warns that some permission choices
can affect which beacons are visible. Neither a general Bluetooth setting nor a
single collector API promises a complete packet stream.
[cite:@bluetooth-primer-owned;@android-bluetooth-permissions-owned]

Therefore distinguish:

- configured advertising behavior, if supplied by the enrolled device's
  documented configuration;
- observed inter-arrival intervals at a specified receiver;
- deduplicated analytical observation intervals;
- ingestion inter-arrival intervals after batching or retries.

If three observations reach storage together, that may be an upload batch
rather than a burst of radio activity. Use acquisition time when describing
the observation sequence and ingestion time when measuring pipeline latency.

A long gap can justify investigating the collection chain. It does not by
itself diagnose a depleted battery, deliberate shutdown, movement or a changed
owner behavior.

** Summarize signals under comparable conditions

For a single receiver pose and configuration, median RSSI and a spread measure
can describe the supplied observations. A robust spread summary may be more
informative than a mean alone when a small number of values are unusual.

Always include the sample count and the filtering rule. A summary from three
samples has different evidential weight from one observed across many
independent placements and sessions. Many adjacent packets from one static
condition should not be mistaken for broad environmental coverage.

Do not pool measurements indiscriminately across:

- different receiver hardware;
- different antenna orientation;
- different cart stops;
- materially different obstructions;
- changed transmit-power configuration;
- different scanning or filtering settings.

You may report those conditions together in a comparative table. The labels
must remain visible so a difference is not automatically attributed to the
asset.

A profile can say that the distribution changed after a firmware update.
Establishing that the update caused the change requires a controlled comparison
or other supporting evidence. The timeline alone does not identify the cause.

** Profile movement as supported state changes

The companion dataset includes accepted west-bay and east-bay windows,
deliberate coverage gaps, an unresolved radio alias and a covered interval with
no accepted sightings.

The intended report grid is:

| Report time | Intended evidence situation |
|-------------+-----------------------------|
| 100, 110 | repeated west-bay support |
| 120, 130 | repeated east-bay support |
| 140 | receiver coverage gap |
| 150 | radio alias without an applicable enrollment binding |
| 160, 170 | west-bay support through an explicitly enrolled rotated alias |
| 180 | coverage present, no accepted sightings |
| 190, 200 | renewed west-bay support |
| 210, 220 | repeated east-bay support |

These are declared synthetic test cases. They are not measurements from a
real Bluetooth installation.

Under the specified confirmation policy, acquisition or reacquisition differs
from a continuously supported change. An unknown interval resets continuity.
A new confirmed label after that interval should not imply the path or state
through the gap.

The fixture's target behavior includes first acquisition at 110, a confirmed
change at 130, reacquisition at 170, reacquisition at 200, and another confirmed
change at 220. This sequence is the test's expected reasoning target; use the
actual execution report to establish whether an implementation produced it.

Reacquisition at the same zone does not establish continuous occupancy. The
asset might have stayed, moved and returned, or remained unobserved for some
other reason. The supplied evidence does not choose among those explanations.

** Keep a behavioral vocabulary appropriate to equipment

Useful profile statements include:

- "observed in twelve receiver records after exact deduplication";
- "zone estimate unavailable in these named windows";
- "last accepted observation acquired at this experiment time";
- "a different address was linked only after an explicit enrollment update";
- "signal spread changed under a different receiver pose";
- "ingestion delay increased while acquisition intervals remained similar."

Each statement points to measurements or a declared processing rule.

Avoid statements that outrun the evidence, such as "the asset was switched
off" when only sightings are absent, or "the equipment followed this path" when
only separated zones were supported.

A device's association with a person does not authorize a profile of that
person. Keep the enrollment scope, ownership and purpose with the experiment.
If the requested question changes from equipment behavior to someone's private
routine, that is a different task and requires a different assessment.

** Evaluate profiles with ground truth kept outside the model

Calibration teaches the model what the exercise's supported zones look like.
Held-out ground truth evaluates its outputs. Do not feed the evaluation answers
back into the model while claiming they remain an independent test.

Separate whole sessions or placement conditions when adjacent samples share
context. Splitting nearly identical neighboring rows across calibration and
evaluation can make the test unrealistically easy.

Freeze the model and thresholds before the final evaluation. If a result
changes because you adjusted the margin threshold after inspecting mistakes,
record that as development work and evaluate the revised model on different
held-out cases.

Useful evaluation questions include:

1. Which labeled windows received an accepted zone?
2. Among those accepted windows, how many labels were correct?
3. Which true zones were confused with others?
4. How often did the model abstain?
5. How did performance change with a missing receiver or an altered pose?
6. How often did a transition rule react to noise?
7. How long did confirmation delay a real scripted change?
8. How much acquisition-to-ingestion latency did the pipeline add?

Metrics should expose failures as well as successful classifications. Report
unclassified windows as unclassified; do not remove them silently from the
denominator used to claim overall usefulness.

** A small error-metric worksheet

Consider ten fictional eligible windows. A method accepts eight labels, of
which seven match the held-out ground truth.

#+begin_example
accepted-label accuracy = 7 / 8
zone-output availability = 8 / 10
correct accepted output per eligible window = 7 / 10
#+end_example

These ratios answer different questions. The first alone makes the method
look better than a report that also shows its abstentions.

Suppose a second method accepts all ten windows and labels seven correctly.
Both methods produce seven correct accepted outputs, but one abstains twice
while the other makes three wrong assignments. Which is preferable depends on
the cost of an unknown answer versus a wrong answer. State that decision
context instead of presenting one metric as universally decisive.

For transition metrics, define an event-matching rule before scoring:
which scripted change corresponds to which reported change, how much timing
tolerance is allowed, and how unmatched detections are counted. Do not award
multiple true positives to repeated reports of one scripted transition.

For continuous position estimates, retain the localization error distribution
and the estimate-availability denominator. The zone baseline in this lab does
not emit precise positions, so it does not have a measured metre-level
localization score to advertise.

** Express the result in canonical records

Use an =asset= record for enrolled equipment identity. A receiver is equipment
too, but its role as observer must remain distinct from the measured subject.

Use =observation= records for individual or explicitly grouped measurements,
and an =analysis= record for the derived profile. The current canonical
=analysis= vocabulary includes =inputIds=, =method=, =findings=, =conclusions=,
=limitations=, =uncertaintySources= and =unresolved=.
[cite:@starlang-starintel-0101]

The lab's canonical fixtures are validated through the same pinned SDK as the
earlier book exercises. Their outer experiment packet is a course container,
not a new StarIntel dtype.

A useful analysis describes:

- the enrolled subject and observation interval;
- the method and parameter version;
- which observations and calibration records it used;
- the supported zone or visibility findings;
- coverage gaps and excluded inputs;
- limitations and unresolved alternatives;
- the time at which the analysis was produced.

Do not store an estimated local-grid coordinate as a geographic =geo-point=
by renaming x to longitude and y to latitude. The canonical type requires its
declared geometry fields and coordinate semantics. A schema-valid pair of
numbers can still describe the wrong frame.

The separate geographic example in the fixtures is explicitly synthetic and
exists to demonstrate the canonical geotype boundary. It does not georeference
the fictional room or assign a beacon the receiver's position.

** Design an equipment-profile view that shows uncertainty

A useful operator view can show:

- asset identity and enrolled experiment scope;
- latest accepted observation time;
- current display age and stale status;
- supported zone, or unknown;
- receiver coverage for the relevant interval;
- count of distinct supporting observations;
- calibration/model version;
- recent acquisition, change and reacquisition events;
- a link to raw observations and rejection reasons.

Keep receiver trajectories visually distinct from inferred asset states.
Show missing intervals as gaps. Avoid drawing a continuous equipment route
where the system only classified a few separated zones.

A profile exported for another reader should carry the same qualifications.
A screenshot of a green pin without an acquisition timestamp can conceal that
the evidence is old or weak.

This is a proposed view design. The book's offline lab does not implement a
live dashboard, permission service, hardware scanner or StarIntel actor that
performs those functions.

** Failure exercises with answers

1. Two sessions have the same number of sightings, but one has half as much
   receiver uptime. Is their visibility equal?

   The raw counts are equal. Conditional visibility requires the appropriate
   covered-time or covered-window denominator, and wall-clock coverage must
   also be reported.

2. A receiver uploads a batch after reconnecting. What does the ingestion burst
   establish about advertising cadence?

   Very little by itself. Compare acquisition timestamps and deduplicated
   records before interpreting the equipment's observed radio behavior.

3. Reusing one set of observations produces the same zone twice. Does that
   satisfy a two-confirmation transition rule?

   It should not in this lab. Require new eligible supporting observations so
   a repeated computation does not manufacture independent evidence.

4. A previously unseen radio address has a familiar name. What goes into the
   enrolled asset's profile?

   No automatic identity link. Preserve an unresolved observation or exclusion
   according to scope until the trusted enrollment process supplies evidence.

5. The model performs well on rows adjacent to its calibration rows. Has it
   shown generalization across receiver poses?

   No. Evaluate separate placement/pose sessions that actually test that
   difference.

6. A profile reports 95 percent accuracy but omits its abstention count. What
   should you ask for?

   The numerator, denominator, acceptance rule, eligible-window count and
   coverage/unknown breakdown. High accepted-case accuracy may coexist with
   low output availability.

7. A west-bay result appears before a gap and another west-bay result after it.
   How long was the asset continuously in west-bay?

   The evidence does not establish continuous occupancy through the gap.
   Report the observed episodes separately.

8. RSSI falls after a battery replacement. Does that prove the new battery is
   defective?

   No. Check placement, antenna orientation, receiver conditions, configuration
   and other relevant changes before making a causal diagnosis.

9. The canonical analysis record validates. Is its conclusion correct?

   The record meets the selected structural contract. Source quality,
   experimental design, calculations and interpretation still require review.

10. A local x/y estimate is placed into longitude/latitude fields and passes
    range checks. What failed?

    Coordinate semantics. Numerical range validity does not establish a valid
    transformation between reference frames.

** Extension project

Add one new fictional held-out session in which the receiver cart changes
pose and one fixed receiver loses coverage. Keep enrollment bindings and clock
uncertainty explicit. Predict which windows should remain classifiable before
running the code.

Then compare two predeclared acceptance thresholds. Report both accepted-label
accuracy and output availability, preserve all unknown windows, and explain
which tradeoff is appropriate for the equipment-finding task.

Finish with a short analysis record whose claims can be traced back to the
observations. That is the central skill of equipment profiling: useful summaries
that remain honest about how they were measured.
