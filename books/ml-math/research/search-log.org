** 2026-09-16 -- algebraic associativity versus floating-point execution
Question: The manuscript teaches matrix associativity as an algebraic law. What source and executable failure case keep readers from mistaking that law for bit-for-bit invariance under finite-precision regrouping?

Inspected: the official Python 3.14.7 floating-point tutorial, especially its treatment of binary approximation, per-operation rounding, =math.fsum=, and the near-universal IEEE 754 binary64 mapping of Python =float= on current platforms.

Executable check: with one-by-one matrices =A=[[1e308]]=, =B=[[1e-308]]=, =C=[[1e-308]]=, the teaching implementation's two legal parenthesizations follow different finite-precision paths. In the local CPython 3.13.5 development environment, =(AB)C= produced =((1e-308,),)= while =A(BC)= produced =((0.0,),)= because the =B C= intermediate underflowed to zero. The expanded matrix suite passed 18/18 locally; the combined vector/matrix suites passed 32/32. Repository CI on the final branch head remains the authoritative pinned Python 3.14 evidence.

Result: add a dedicated Project 1 numerical failure lab and regression. Claim boundary: the observed decimal outputs are TESTED behavior for the stated runtime, not a claim that every Python implementation or floating-point format must expose the identical boundary. The mathematical associativity claim remains about exact arithmetic; later numerical work will use tolerance-based or condition-aware oracles where appropriate rather than silently replacing all exact tests.
