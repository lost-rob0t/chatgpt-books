* Why matching comes before unification
Pattern matching is the smaller mechanism.  A pattern is compared with a datum in one direction: literal structure must agree, while pattern variables may acquire bindings.  A repeated variable creates an equality constraint.  For example, matching =(same X X)= against =(same a b)= must fail after =X= has already become =a=.

Unification removes that one-way privilege.  Variables may occur on either side, and the algorithm must find one substitution that makes both terms identical when such a substitution exists.  Robinson's 1965 paper introduced the unification machinery used inside first-order resolution and describes the most-general-unifier role of substitution in automated deduction. [cite:@robinson1965]

The book derives the two mechanisms separately so readers can see exactly which obligations appear when moving from matching to unification: dereferencing variable chains, applying substitutions consistently to both sides, detecting incompatible structures, and preventing a variable from being bound to a finite term containing itself.

* Representation contract
The reserved variable representation is:

#+begin_src lisp
(:var x)
#+end_src

or, through the public constructor:

#+begin_src lisp
(make-variable 'x)
#+end_src

This makes variables visible in traces and lets ordinary cons recursion teach the mechanism.  The tradeoff is explicit: =:var= is reserved as a term tag in this project.

Substitutions are association lists from variable names to terms.  This is not chosen for asymptotic performance; it is chosen so a beginner can inspect every binding.  Later optimization work may introduce indexed environments while preserving the semantics established here.

* Finite-term and occurs-check boundary
Milestone A deliberately models finite acyclic first-order terms.  Before binding variable =X= to term =T=, the unifier asks whether =X= occurs in =T= after following the current substitution.  If it does, the result is =:failed= with reason =:occurs-check=.

Example:

#+begin_example
X = f(X)
#+end_example

A cyclic/rational-tree system can assign a different semantics to that equation, but silently switching semantics would make the finite-term derivation false.  Rational trees therefore remain a later research topic, not an optional flag hidden in milestone A.

* Executable acceptance consequences
- Matching is directional and recognizes variables only in the pattern.
- Repeated pattern variables must agree with their earlier binding.
- Both matching and unification recurse over cons cells, so dotted pairs are exercised explicitly.
- Unification dereferences chains before comparing or extending a substitution.
- A successful substitution, when applied to both original terms, must make the results =EQUAL=.
- The occurs check rejects self-containing finite terms.
- Inputs are treated as immutable; substitution application builds fresh cons structure instead of destructively rewriting caller data.
- Failure records preserve a reason and offending term pair/pattern fragment for debugging.
