* Result: composite planner contract
** 1. Separate hard grouping constraints from admissibility constraints
The planner should model two classes of rules.

Hard grouping rules state that two records MUST receive the same split assignment. Initial Project 1 rules:
- same canonical entity identity;
- same exact-duplicate identity;
- same frozen near-duplicate component;
- same source/document lineage component when the lineage means one derived/copied observation could reveal another;
- same explicitly selected graph grouping key when the evaluation question requires generalization across graph regions rather than within one region.

Admissibility rules determine whether a record/features may participate in a particular partition at all:
- temporal cutoff / train-before-test ordering;
- point-in-time feature availability;
- label availability when labels are resolved after observation time;
- any later task-specific embargo/window rule.

A temporal rule therefore must not be faked by merely adding =year= to the grouping key. Time constrains which observations/features are valid; grouping constrains which observations must move together.

** 2. Compile hard grouping rules by equivalence closure
Represent records as vertices in a constraint graph. Add an undirected edge whenever an enabled hard rule says the two records must share a partition. The deterministic composite group is the connected component induced by the transitive closure of those edges.

Canonical component ID rule for Project 1 research harness:
1. normalize record IDs to their canonical manifest string representation;
2. compute connected components over enabled hard-constraint edges;
3. assign each component the lexicographically smallest canonical record ID as =composite_group_id=;
4. emit the full edge provenance that caused each union, rather than retaining only the final opaque group ID.

This means entity grouping and duplicate grouping compose monotonically. If A shares an entity with B and B is a near-duplicate of C, A/B/C are one indivisible split component. No later splitter may separate them.

The implementation may use union-find/disjoint-set or an equivalent connected-component algorithm. The research contract is the resulting equivalence relation and deterministic IDs, not a dependency on a particular graph library.

** 3. Graph grouping is opt-in and task-specific
Do NOT automatically union every pair of StarIntel records connected by any relation. A real investigation graph may contain a giant connected component; blanket graph closure could reduce the entire corpus to one split group and make evaluation impossible.

Graph-derived hard grouping is justified only when the task definition identifies a leakage path that follows a specific relation or bounded structural unit. Examples to research/test later include:
- holding out all observations belonging to one known organization/family/case component;
- keeping edges from the same source document together;
- link-prediction evaluation where reciprocal/duplicate edge facts must not cross partitions.

The manifest must therefore record =graph_group_policy= with the exact relation types, direction handling, traversal depth/component rule, and reason. Default is no graph union beyond other explicitly enabled constraints.

** 4. Apply temporal and point-in-time rules before interpreting scores
For a declared cutoff =T=:
- training observations must satisfy the task's training-time rule;
- test observations must satisfy the task's evaluation-time rule;
- no feature may be emitted for an observation when =feature.available_at > prediction_at=;
- components that straddle an otherwise strict train/test cutoff require an explicit deterministic policy rather than silent row movement.

Proposed first policy for Project 1 research: =reject_straddling_component=. If a hard composite component contains observations on both sides of a strict cutoff, mark the requested split infeasible and emit the offending component/constraints. This is safer for teaching and provenance than silently assigning the whole component to train or test and changing the requested evaluation window.

Alternative policies such as an embargo, dropping pre/post-cutoff rows, or assigning whole components by first/last observation remain task-specific and must be separately declared/tested before use.

** 5. scikit-learn is the splitter, not the policy compiler
Once =composite_group_id= exists, =GroupShuffleSplit= or =GroupKFold= can consume that single group label. Their job is to assign groups; they do not discover entity equivalence, duplicates, lineage, graph leakage, temporal validity, or feature availability for us.

Therefore Project 1 must not pass raw =entity_id= to =GroupShuffleSplit= and pretend the other constraints disappeared. The book layer compiles the domain constraints into one group vector first, then invokes the upstream splitter.

** 6. Required manifest/provenance fields
The dataset-build/split manifest should record at minimum:
- planner schema/version owned by Book 1;
- deterministic seed when a randomized group splitter is used;
- enabled hard-constraint rule names and parameters;
- exact duplicate normalization/hash rule;
- near-duplicate algorithm, threshold, and candidate-generation version;
- source/document lineage rule;
- graph grouping policy or explicit =none=;
- component-ID algorithm;
- temporal cutoff/window and timezone convention;
- point-in-time feature-availability policy;
- straddling-component policy;
- upstream splitter class + version-sensitive parameters;
- counts of rows, entities, composite groups and partitions;
- contamination diagnostics and infeasibility diagnostics;
- immutable source/fixture identities already required by earlier provenance passes.

StarIntel document validation/serialization remains behind the existing Book 1 StarIntel adapter. CouchDB revisions, queues, RabbitMQ routing, actor supervision, durable ingest and server search are not part of this manifest and must stay in =starintel-server=.

* Required contamination diagnostics before model evaluation
A split result is not ready for scoring until the harness reports these diagnostics:
- entity IDs shared across train/test;
- exact hashes shared across train/test;
- near-duplicate component IDs shared across train/test;
- source-lineage component IDs shared across train/test;
- enabled graph-group IDs shared across train/test;
- hard composite group IDs shared across train/test;
- maximum training observation/prediction time and minimum test observation/prediction time for strict temporal tasks;
- count of emitted features whose availability exceeds their prediction time (must be zero);
- count/list of cutoff-straddling composite groups;
- per-partition class/label counts when labels exist;
- number and proportion of rows excluded by admissibility rules.

The first six overlap counts must be zero for their enabled hard constraints. A nonzero result is a split-construction failure, not an interesting model result.

* Results
The foundational design gate is narrower now. Project 1 no longer needs to invent precedence rules such as "entity beats duplicate beats time." Hard must-link constraints compose by transitive closure; temporal and feature-availability rules remain explicit admissibility constraints; upstream grouped splitters receive one compiled group vector.

The design also preserves the StarIntel boundary: the research pipeline owns dataset/split provenance and canonical adapter calls, while =starintel-server= continues to own runtime persistence, routing and dataflow semantics.

* Remaining uncertainty
- The proposed =reject_straddling_component= policy has not yet been exercised on factual public data.
- Near-duplicate candidate generation remains O(n^2) in the small deterministic control; scalable MinHash/LSH or embedding-assisted candidate generation is still unresolved.
- A real public fixture large enough to exercise entity + lineage + time simultaneously must be selected without implying its size supports meaningful production performance estimation.
- Task-specific graph grouping rules for relationship ML/link prediction remain a later-project research question.
- Cross-language StarIntel conformance and the final transitive dependency lock remain separate work.
