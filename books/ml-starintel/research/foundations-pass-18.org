* Teaching mechanism
Chapter 6 is deliberately layered:

1. reuse Chapter 1's training-mean constant baseline;
2. derive one-feature OLS from centered sums;
3. fail closed when the feature has zero variance;
4. generalize the same residual-squared objective to multiple features;
5. use scikit-learn =LinearRegression= only after the mechanism is explicit;
6. evaluate OLS and the baseline on the exact same held-out groups;
7. derive MAE, RMSE, and R^2 from residuals;
8. inspect predicted-vs-actual and residual plots;
9. inject one large held-out target error without retraining;
10. issue a deliberately out-of-range prediction but refuse to score it without a label;
11. duplicate one training predictor exactly, inspect dense-design rank/singular values, and demonstrate that individual duplicate-feature coefficients are not separately identifiable even when held-out predictions remain equivalent.

* Exact-duplicate-predictor hardening protocol
The coefficient-identifiability addition was frozen before inspecting a pinned-environment result.

The new probe must:
1. keep the original three-feature fit unchanged;
2. create a separate four-column diagnostic design by appending an exact copy of =token_count=;
3. fit that augmented design on the exact same training rows and targets;
4. require the original design to have full column rank on this frozen control;
5. require the duplicated design to have rank lower than its feature count;
6. compare predictions from the original and duplicated designs on the exact same held-out rows with =rtol= and =atol= both =1e-10=;
7. record the maximum absolute prediction delta;
8. require the two duplicated =token_count= coefficients to sum to the original =token_count= coefficient within test tolerance;
9. record singular values for both dense designs;
10. make no assertion about the exact ratio in which a solver allocates coefficient weight between the two identical columns.

The algebraic reason is independent of the solver. If two columns are both =x=, their prediction contribution is =b1*x + b2*x = (b1+b2)*x=. Changing =b1= by =+c= and =b2= by =-c= leaves every prediction unchanged. The individual duplicate-column coefficients are therefore not identified by those data.

This is an identifiability/debugging probe, not a new performance benchmark. It does not establish the magnitude of coefficient instability under merely near-collinear real-world features; that remains a separate future experiment.

* Synthetic-control contract
The executable =chapter06_regression.py= creates a deterministic Tier C control:

- 12 synthetic case groups;
- 3 records per case;
- 8 groups declared training and 4 held out;
- three features: =token_count=, =relation_count=, =source_count=;
- one synthetic numeric target: =review_minutes=;
- target generation: a known linear rule plus a small deterministic error term.

The names make the example concrete, but the target is not observed analyst behavior. The report labels its evidence scope as a deterministic Tier C synthetic regression control.

* Executable invariants
The focused test suite requires:

1. exact one-feature line recovery;
2. fail-closed zero-variance behavior;
3. exact first-principles metric values on a tiny known vector, including negative R^2;
4. 24 training + 12 test rows with no group overlap;
5. lower held-out RMSE for OLS than the training-only mean on the deliberately linear frozen control;
6. held-out R^2 above 0.9 only as a fixture regression invariant, not a production threshold;
7. mutation of held-out targets cannot change fitted intercept/coefficients;
8. the frozen +60-minute outlier changes RMSE more than MAE;
9. the extrapolation probe is outside the observed training feature range and is not scored because no label exists;
10. an exact duplicate predictor makes the augmented dense design rank-deficient while preserving the held-out prediction vector within the declared tolerance and preserving the original predictor's combined coefficient contribution.
